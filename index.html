Mewni Alahakoon

I study how everyday hardware — contactless payment cards, USB and Thunderbolt peripherals — can be attacked, and how to measure and defend against those attacks. My interests also extend to zero-day vulnerability and exploit ecosystems, social engineering and cyberwarfare.

Now Independent research on USB and peripheral-interface security, in collaboration with Dr. Asanka P. Sayakkara (UCSC, University of Colombo).

Research experience

2026 – Present

Independent Researcher

USB and Peripheral-Interface Security

In collaboration with Dr. Asanka P. Sayakkara, UCSC, University of Colombo · Colombo, Sri Lanka

  • Conducting an ongoing systematic literature review of USB security [3], covering USB4, USB-C Power Delivery, Thunderbolt, and malicious cable implants.
  • Developing an attack-layer × attacker-capability taxonomy that spans the physical to the application layer, and surveying host-side defences such as device authorisation, firmware signing and kernel DMA protection.
  • Mapping open problems where attack classes remain unstudied or proposed defences lack independent evaluation.

2024 – 2025

Postgraduate Researcher

Master’s Thesis

UCSC, University of Colombo · Supervisor: Dr. Asanka P. Sayakkara · Colombo, Sri Lanka

  • Led the first systematic, cross-provider security assessment of NFC payment cards issued in Sri Lanka, covering cards from nine banks under a defined proximity-attacker threat model [1].
  • Designed an original quantitative security-scoring model combining read success, read latency, distance resistance and wallet-shielding resistance across 11 experimental scenarios.
  • Ran data-extraction (Flipper Zero), jamming (nine cards) and replay (five cards) experiments at 13.56 MHz with HackRF One and GNU Radio; jamming was defeated by EMV anti-collision and signal modulation, while replay was limited by SDR frequency drift.
  • Showed that the principal risk lay in the wider payment ecosystem rather than in card cryptography: card data exposed over NFC could be misused where online merchants apply incomplete validation.

2025

Postgraduate Researcher

Zero-Day Exploits in Cyberwarfare

UCSC, University of Colombo · Sole-authored study [2] · Colombo, Sri Lanka

  • Conducted a qualitative comparative case study of Stuxnet, WannaCry and Pegasus, using thematic coding of state motives, offence–defence imbalance and security impact.
  • Showed how stockpiling and the exploit market create an offence–defence asymmetry; assessed governance gaps against the U.S. Vulnerabilities Equities Process and the Tallinn Manual 2.0.

2021 – 2022

Undergraduate Researcher

BSc Thesis

University of Plymouth (delivered at NSBM Green University) · Supervisor: Mr. Chamindra Attanayake · Homagama, Sri Lanka

  • Designed a real-time, multi-stage detection architecture for zero-day ransomware on Windows, combining pre-execution static analysis of downloaded executables with behavioural monitoring of file, directory and registry activity.
  • Implemented the static-analysis stage as a two-model pipeline: a random-forest classifier over PE-file features and a convolutional neural network trained on 9,639 malware images from 25 families (Microsoft malware dataset).
  • Deployed both models as a FastAPI service (Python, scikit-learn, TensorFlow/Keras).
